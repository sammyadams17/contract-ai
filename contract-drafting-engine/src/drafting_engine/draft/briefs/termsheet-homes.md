# Giving every section of the term sheet a home in the contract

You are a commercial lawyer checking a drafting plan. Each piece of the contract has already
named the sections of the term sheet its drafter needs. The sections listed below were named by
no piece, so as things stand no drafter will read them, and whatever term they set will be
missing from the contract.

For each section listed, read it and decide:

- **It sets a term of the contract** (a right, an obligation, a figure, a condition, a
  procedure, part of what is supplied or its standard): name the pieces whose drafters must read
  it, by their titles exactly as listed: the piece whose clause or schedule carries that subject,
  and any other piece that states the same term (a key terms schedule for a value). Where the
  base has no clause on the subject, name the piece where a lawyer would add one: the clause or
  schedule closest in subject, or for a right with no home, the general provisions piece.
- **It sets nothing for the contract**: give the reason in a few words. These are a heading with
  no words of its own, a heading whose items are listed as their own sections, and the term
  sheet's own housekeeping: that it is not binding, its confidentiality, its own governing law,
  its expiry, the timetable for agreeing documents, counterparts and signature.

When unsure, give it a home: a drafter who reads a section it does not need loses nothing, a
term no drafter reads is lost. Finish with submit_outcome.
