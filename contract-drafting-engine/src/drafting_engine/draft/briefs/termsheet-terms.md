# One home for each term of a term sheet section

You are a commercial lawyer checking a drafting plan. Several pieces of the contract will read
the section of the term sheet below, and their drafters work at the same time without seeing
each other's work. A term written by each of them is written several times. Give each term the
section sets one piece that writes it.

List each term the section sets (a right, an obligation, a figure, a condition, a definition),
in a few words naming it, and the piece that writes it, its title copied exactly from the pieces
listed:

- the piece whose clause or schedule holds the like provision as the base now stands, read from
  what each piece holds: where the base already has a clause on the term's subject, its piece
  writes the term, and no other piece adds a second provision or definition for it;
- where the term sheet has a schedule of its own (a specification, a service description, a
  payment table) that replaces one in the base, every term it sets is written by the piece
  holding that schedule, and no other piece writes it;
- never a piece holding only an intentionally blank clause or schedule;
- a new defined term in the definitions piece whose range of the alphabet holds it, and one
  that sorts between two pieces' ranges in the earlier of them;
- a term that gives what a defined term means (who a party is, a figure the definition
  states): the definitions piece whose range holds that definition, and a clause piece as well
  only where its clause states the same value in its own words;
- a figure a key terms schedule also shows: the clause piece, and the key terms piece as a
  second entry for the same term;
- a term with no like provision in the base: the piece closest in subject;
- a term that by its nature stands in several places (a party's name wherever the contract
  prints it): one entry for each place, each with its piece;
- a rule the term sheet itself says changes words wherever they stand in the contract: the piece
  `every piece`. An obligation, a cost, a right or a condition is written once, in one piece,
  never `every piece`.

Every other piece reads the section for context only. A section that sets no term of the
contract (a heading, the term sheet's own housekeeping) gets no entries, and neither does a term
spent at or before the contract's signing: conditions to signing, the documentation date, and
what happens if no contract is agreed by then. Finish with submit_outcome.
